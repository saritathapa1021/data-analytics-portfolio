# U.S. Mental Health During COVID-19 (2020–2024)

**Tableau | Data Visualization | Public Health Analytics | Dashboard Design**

## Project Overview

Mental health became a major public health concern during and after the COVID-19 pandemic. This project analyzes anxiety and depression trends across the United States using CDC Household Pulse Survey data from 2020–2024.

Using Tableau, I developed an interactive dashboard to explore national trends, geographic disparities, demographic differences, and changes in mental health indicators over time. The goal was to transform complex survey data into actionable insights for public health stakeholders.

---

## Business Problem

Public health organizations require timely insights to understand how mental health outcomes vary across populations and regions.

This project addresses questions such as:

- How did anxiety and depression rates change throughout the pandemic?
- Which states reported the highest symptom levels?
- Which demographic groups were most affected?
- Are anxiety symptoms generally higher than depression symptoms?
- How did state rankings change over time?

## Key Findings

### National Trends

Mental health symptoms peaked during late 2020 and early 2021 and gradually declined by 2024, suggesting a gradual recovery from the most disruptive stages of the pandemic.

### Geographic Disparities

Mental health outcomes varied significantly across states, with some states consistently reporting higher symptom rates than others. These differences highlight uneven mental health burdens across the United States.

### Anxiety vs. Depression

Anxiety symptoms were generally reported at higher rates than depression symptoms. This pattern may reflect stronger short-term responses to uncertainty and stress during the pandemic period.

### Demographic Insights

Younger adults consistently reported higher levels of anxiety and depression compared with other demographic groups.

### State Ranking Changes

States did not remain permanently fixed in the same positions over time. Rankings shifted throughout the survey period, indicating evolving regional impacts and recovery patterns.

---

## Recommendations

Based on the analysis:

- Prioritize mental health outreach programs for younger adults.
- Monitor states with consistently elevated symptom rates.
- Consider confidence intervals when interpreting small differences.
- Continue tracking mental health indicators to identify emerging trends.

---

## Limitations

- Survey responses are self-reported.
- Findings do not represent clinical diagnoses.
- Survey periods are not daily observations.
- Some comparisons contain statistical uncertainty.
- Results identify associations, not causation.
